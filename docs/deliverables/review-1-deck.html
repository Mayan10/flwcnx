Review 1

Predictive Bandwidth Allocation for Starlink Access Links

A regime-conditioned calibration layer for risk-aware throughput forecasting over LEO satellite networks.

Mayan Sharma, Kriti Saini, Devansh Behl01 / 17

Domain

A link that is non-stationary by construction

  • 550km orbit, not 36,000 like geostationary
  • 7.5km/s, so a satellite is usable for minutes
  • ~30ms latency, down from 600 plus

Speed is bought with constant handover. That trade is the entire domain.

Domain and problem02 / 17

Domain

Five mechanisms drive the variation

  • 115-second scheduling cycle
  • 2Satellite handovers at each boundary
  • 3Serving geometry: elevation and distance
  • 4Candidate density: visible satellites
  • 5Weather and obstruction

Each one becomes a feature, and later a regime axis.

Domain and problem03 / 17

Problem statement

Six industry needs. We address three.

  • 1Predict throughput degradation
  • 2Detect congestion before users are affected
  • 3Optimise bandwidth allocation automatically

Chosen because they chain into one system, not three disconnected models.

Proposed method

Calibrate inside each operating regime

  • 115 s phase: opening, middle, closing
  • 2Elevation: 25 to 45, 45 to 60, above 60°
  • 3Distance: below or above 645 km
  • 4Candidate count: terciles

Covariates the terminal already has at prediction time. Never the future.

Methodology11 / 17

Methodology

The procedure

  • 1Split temporally: train, calibration, test
  • 2Fit the forecaster on train only
  • 3Residuals on calibration, grouped by regime
  • 4Select each regime's operating point
  • 5At test time, assign regime and apply

Sparse regimes climb a coarsening hierarchy, and we report how much mass falls back.

System design

Five layers, strictly downward

ingest/raw sources to normalised frame
state/frame to features and regime id
forecast/features to point prediction
calibrate/prediction to safe bound · our contribution
decide/bound to allocation and alerts
System design13 / 17

System design

Congestion is derived, not modelled

The calibrated bound falling below the committed allocation for W sustained slots is the congestion signal.

No second classifier. The alert and the allocator can never disagree about when the link is in trouble.

System design14 / 17

Evaluation

Success and failure, defined in advance

  • YGlobal rate at or below budget
  • YConditional rate on P30 and P10 well below 0.65 to 0.86
  • YAt comparable MAE
  • NIf accuracy collapses: negative result, reported

Pre-specifying failure is what makes the success claim worth anything.
